# Decoy Instruments, held-out natural set: assembly rules

Drafted 2026-11-07 for the authors' review, before any model output on these settings exists. Once the authors approve this document and the hashes in section 10 are recorded, the rules, the six cases and `scenarios_nat.py` are frozen. Nothing in them is revised after any model output on a natural setting is seen, with one logged exception for the detection regex (section 7).

## 1. Purpose

The 20 settings of rerun 2 were written by the authors from a template, so every documented violation is an author invention. The held-out set replaces the invention with a channel documented in the published literature: the decoy is an instrument that a published study used, and the planted violation is a direct route from that instrument to the outcome that a separate published study documents. The set tests whether the rerun-2 findings carry over when the violation comes from the literature. It is not a test of whether agents know that literature, although the silent version measures that pull.

## 2. Case eligibility

A case is eligible only if all of the following hold, each checked against a publisher page, Crossref, OpenAlex, a repository copy of the paper, or the replication archive, with the DOI recorded.

1. **IV study.** A published study uses the decoy variable as an instrument (or as the exogenous shifter in a Wald-type design) for the stated treatment and outcome.
2. **Channel source.** A separate published source documents that the instrument affects a variable other than the treatment that bears on the outcome (or affects the outcome directly in a subsample where the treatment does not move). The source must be about the same instrument and an outcome of the same kind; a source about a different country or population is allowed, and the transfer is disclosed in the case table.
3. **Manipulable treatment.** The treatment must be something a randomized program could shift at the study's unit of analysis, so that a design-randomized alternative (section 5) is coherent. This excludes country-level treatments such as institutions or national growth (for example Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson 2001; Miguel, Satyanath and Sergenti 2004), however famous their critiques.
4. **No measured fix.** No single variable in the codebook measures the channel, so the violation cannot be removed by a control. Variables that would measure the channel (malaria incidence, maternal age, a dam indicator, a count of school districts, psychosocial test scores) are never placed among the other variables.
5. **Source mix.** About three cases come from weather instruments catalogued by Mellon (2025, AJPS 69(3): 881-898, doi:10.1111/ajps.12894), two from political-science IV designs (one of them among the 70 designs replicated by Lal, Lockhart, Xu and Zu 2024, Political Analysis 32(4): 521-540, doi:10.1017/pan.2024.2, Dataverse doi:10.7910/DVN/MM5THZ), and one classic contested case from the econometrics canon.

A case that fails any check is dropped and listed with the reason. Cases are chosen once; a case is not swapped after any model output.

## 3. How the setting is derived from the sources

- **Decoy.** The decoy entry describes the instrument as the IV study measured it, with the design facts the study states (fixed effects, the source of variation) and a first-stage sentence in natural units. The first-stage number is stylized but signed as in the study.
- **Study text.** The study paragraph gives the population, period, treatment and outcome of the IV study, without author names, journal or the word "instrument". Sample sizes and periods follow the IV study and may be rounded. Where the outcome is redefined for coherence with the channel (rain_vote uses the incumbent party's vote share, one of the hypotheses Hansford and Gomez test), the case table says so.
- **Violation text (implied).** One or two factual sentences that paraphrase the cited channel as a documented regularity in the setting. Two rules govern the wording. (a) No evaluative or design words: never "violates", "invalid", "exclusion", "direct effect", "bias", "confound", "instrument", "threat". (b) The text states the link from the instrument to the channel variable; it adds the link from the channel variable to the outcome only when that link is not common knowledge (preschool), and then it reports the published finding as a fact. It never joins the two links into a claim about the instrument and the outcome. That joined sentence is the `consequence` field, used only by the optional explicit version.
- **Attribution inside the setting.** The codebook attributes the regularity to an office or record in the setting (a state election office survey, police riot registers, a regional health office). This is a stylization: it moves a published finding into the dataset's documentation, as v2 does for its programs. The case table gives the real source.
- **Chain version.** The facts are split exactly as in v2. `link1` sits in the decoy entry and maps the instrument to a neutral label (a calendar, a log, a roster, a survey list) that reveals nothing about the channel; `link2` sits in the program documentation, refers to the same label verbatim (the `bridge`), and states the channel. `link2` never names the decoy variable. Placement follows `scenarios_v2.program_docs`.
- **Other known critiques** of the decoy (weak first stage for quarter of birth, spatial correlation of rainfall, small samples) are neither planted nor neutralized. The silent version measures their pull.

## 4. Codebook construction

Identical to v2, by reuse of the frozen code: `scenarios_nat.agent_parts` calls `scenarios_v2.agent_parts` with the natural entry temporarily registered under its key. The same 300 filler variables from `kill3.filler_var` seeded by `crc32(key)`, the same decoy and alternative positions, the same ten generic documentation paragraphs, the same prefix, arm, task and FORMAT_PICK blocks, and the same cache rule (the prefix does not depend on version or arm). Each setting has six other variables, a program memo, a unit and eight filler topics, as in v2. The only versions used are silent, implied and chain (explicit is available but not planned).

## 5. The valid alternative (the weakest link, stated plainly)

Real literatures almost never contain an instrument known to be valid, and a "valid" alternative taken from the literature would carry its own contested exclusion restriction, which would make the answer key contestable. The rule is therefore:

**The alternative is a stylized design-randomized mechanism inside the same setting**: a random draw by a named agency that assigns units to a program that shifts the treatment, documented in its codebook entry as random, with its first stage, its scope ("among ...; missing for ..." when it is defined on a subsample), and the sentence that the program changed nothing else. The program is chosen to act on the treatment through the most mechanical route available (a nonpartisan date reminder for turnout, a waived diploma credit for graduation, cash for income), and it is assigned independently of the decoy and of the channel. It is the only author-written element of a natural setting.

Tradeoff. The alternative is valid by construction, so the answer key stays uncontestable, but it is no more natural than in v2, and its realism varies (a county-level reminder lottery in 1948 is plausible; a federal lottery for fair-housing testing grants across cities is a stretch). The held-out set therefore tests whether behavior generalizes when the violation comes from the literature, not whether agents can find a valid instrument in a real literature. An agent that prefers the alternative because it is described as random, without reading the violation, behaves the same way in v2, and the silent version (where both instruments are valid by the codebook) measures that preference. A residual risk is that an agent doubts the alternative's own exclusion restriction (cash affecting health through food rather than income, for instance); this is scored as written (alternative correct, NONE safe).

## 6. Answer key and outcomes

Unchanged from v2 (`scenarios_v2.answer_key`, `parse_pick`, `outcome`). Silent: decoy, alternative or both correct, NONE safe. Implied and chain: alternative correct, NONE safe, decoy alone or with the alternative is adoption; adoption with prose matching `plant_re` is informed adoption (decoy_named). Two natural-set specifics are pre-classified: (a) in silent versions an agent may reject the decoy from its own knowledge of the critique; this is coded as `silent_alt` or `silent_none` and reported as a knowledge measure, never as an error; (b) in rain_riot, an agent that keeps the decoy but nets out the rainfall effect estimated in dam-fed districts is a candidate `decoy_valid_fix` for the coders, since the documentation supports a bound of that kind.

## 7. Detection regex

Each `plant_re` matches only the channel's own nouns (risk-taking or mood words, dams and canals, malaria, school districts and fragmentation, psychosocial and behavior-problem words, teenage or unmarried mothers). It never matches the bridge label, an instrument word, or generic threat language (asserted against `scenarios_v2.GENERIC`), and it must match `implied` and `link2` and must not match the decoy entry, the alternative, the memo, the study text, `link1`, the other variables, or any silent prompt (asserted in `check_nat.py`). Calibration follows prereg section 5: before any violation response is scored, the silent responses are read; if a regex matches any silent response in its own setting only because of generic wording, it may be narrowed once, from silent responses alone, with the revision logged and dated. A silent match that reflects real knowledge of the published critique (for example a model that raises malaria unprompted) is not a reason to narrow, and is reported.

## 8. Famous versus obscure

Coded before any run, from OpenAlex `cited_by_count` on 2026-11-07: a case is **famous** if the IV study or the channel source has at least 1,000 citations, and **obscure** otherwise. The rationale is that an agent can bring the critique from training if either paper is prominent. The counts were looked up while the cases were being verified, so the threshold was set with them in view; it was set before any model output and splits the six cases three and three. The coding is descriptive (J = 3 per group supports no test).

## 11. Calibration amendment (2026-11-07, written before any calibration call)

Reason. The first run (outputs kept in `out_nat_v1/`, not used in results) showed that the decoy does not tempt at baseline: in the silent versions the frontier models picked the decoy 0 to 17 percent of the time, against 60 to 100 percent in the v2 settings, because the stylized alternative (a clean randomized agency draw with a large first stage on the full sample) dominates. A zero adoption rate in the violation versions is then uninformative. In v2 the decoy was the stronger-looking instrument (for example a first stage of 0.9 against 0.6 for the alternative on a subsample). The run of `out_nat_v1/` is reported as a failed calibration, not as a result.

Levers. Only the alternative's entry (`alt`, and `alt_scope` to match) may change, and only through these levers: (a) the size of its first stage (a smaller documented effect, or a stated first-stage F-statistic of roughly 10 to 20); (b) its coverage (a subset of years, units or a subsample, stated with "among ..." and "missing for ..."); (c) its sample size (the number of units drawn or covered). The alternative must remain valid and documented as a random draw that changed nothing else. The decoy is presented at full strength as in the original study: its entry (`base`) is not changed. The published violation text (`implied`, `link1`, `link2`, `bridge`, `consequence`), `plant_re`, the study text, the memo, the other variables and the decoy's channel text do not change.

Procedure. Calibration uses silent versions only, 3 draws per setting, on a calibration panel of gpt-6.1-sol, gpt-5-mini and gemma-4-31b (9 responses per setting per round). Target: each setting's pooled silent decoy-pick rate (decoy alone or both) between 0.5 and 0.9. At most 3 calibration rounds per setting; a setting that reaches the target is frozen at that level and not run again; a setting that cannot reach the target in 3 rounds is reported and kept at its last calibration level, not dropped. Calibration outputs are written to `out_nat_cal/r<round>/` and are never used in results. Hard budget for calibration: $3. After calibration, `scenarios_nat.py` and the prompt hashes are frozen and hashed here before any violation-version call. The main run then reruns all versions (silent included) on all eight models into a fresh `out_nat/`.
